Seven chapters, and one before them

Learning Tock from the Ground Up

Microcontrollers and the Tock kernel from first principles, written from the position of someone actually learning them rather than someone who already knows.

Chapter 1 asks one question: any code can write any address, so what stops it? Every chapter after it answers part of that, and the last one lands on grants, which is the final mechanism chapter 1 names. The arc closes where the first chapter said it would.

How to read a claim here

Nothing on these pages asks to be believed

Claims about the kernel are checked against the Tock tree and cited by file, and wherever a chapter's argument turns on a particular line, it gives the line and the commit it is pinned to. Where a chapter rests on the RP2350 or Pico 2 datasheet instead, it says so, and gives the section wherever a fact turns on one.

Three chapters ship the source a figure rests on, so that what those figures print is compiled or assembled rather than transcribed — listings in chapters 1 and 6, and in chapter 7 a byte count. That is not a stylistic preference: the first draft of that byte count was wrong by four bytes, and no amount of careful reading had caught it.

Why the wall before the door

Problems first, mechanisms second

The memory protection unit comes before syscalls: meet the wall, then find the door. It is the same shape as letting a read-modify-write race lose a pin before the atomic register is offered — the mechanism means nothing until you have felt the problem it answers.

Hardware

Groundable on a board you can hold

Every chapter cites boards/raspberry_pi_pico_2, a real port in this tree, so a reader with a Pico 2 and a debug probe can run what they have just read about.

If your board is a Pico 2 W

It is not that board. This tree has no raspberry_pi_pico_2_w, and the _w board it does have is built on an earlier chip. The chapters say so where it costs something — most sharply that GPIO 25 is the radio's chip select rather than the LED, so the panic blink lands somewhere you cannot see it.

Verifying

The series lives inside the tree it describes

Keeping the chapters in a branch of the Tock repository means every citation can be checked against the exact commit the branch sits on — and when a rebase moves a line a chapter quoted, that is information rather than an inconvenience. The pages are interactive, so "it looks right" is not evidence. From the repository root:

python3 learning/tools/check.py
